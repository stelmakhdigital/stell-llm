The warehouse district wakes before dawn. Crates of oranges ride the forklift
paths, and the morning clerk logs every arrival in a ledger kept since 1998.
Nobody remembers who started the ledger, but everybody follows the same rule:
two lines per crate, one for weight and one for origin. The rule survived three
managers, a flood, and a rumour that the port would close. It did not close.
The oranges go north by truck, the lemons stay, and the ledger grows by two
lines at a time. In the afternoon a student asks why the ledger matters. The
clerk answers that measurement is a promise: the same scale, the same words,
the same two lines. When the flood came, the ledger was carried to the roof in
a plastic bag, and after the water fell back, the lines were rewritten in the
same order. Promise, then, is the habit of writing two lines per crate.
